# Version 2 roadmap

Improvements and features for the next version, drawn from a five-reviewer audit of v1 (accuracy, planning
completeness, competitive features, first-time-user clarity, robustness). Nothing here is implemented yet.

Items are in **one list ordered by criticality**, across all kinds of work:

- **P0** — the app gives a wrong or dangerously misleading answer that a user would act on.
- **P1** — materially changes a typical user's answer, or silently breaks on a plausible input.
- **P2** — a noticeable gap for many users, or a wrong answer in a narrower case.
- **P3** — polish, auditability, rare edge cases.

Within each tier, items are ordered by how much they move a user's answer. Numbers come from runs of the v1
engine on the example plan (plan start 2026, 10,000 simulated markets, seed 20260924) unless marked
*estimate*. The deferred list in [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md#deferred-to-v2) is folded in here.

## P0 — wrong or dangerously misleading

| # | Item | Kind | Evidence | Minimal change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Deflate tax-basis amounts each year.** Taxable basis, Roth contributions and Roth conversion amounts are fixed nominal amounts but are carried in today's dollars without deflating. Capital gains come out understated and early Roth access overstated. | Accuracy | $100k held 20 yrs at 5% real / 3% inflation: gain share 62.3% instead of 79.1%. Example plan Traditional moves **2039 → 2040** once fixed. | After the growth step divide `taxableBasis`, `rothPrincipal[i]`, `conversions[i][*]` by `1 + inflation`; give `projectState` average inflation. Deterministic test on the 79.1% case. |
| 2 | **Flag example values still in use.** Only People is open by default; the other sections keep example numbers with no warning once results show. | Trust | A 35-year-old couple who edit only People is told **"You can stop saving now"**, based on the example's $880k. With a real $60k saved: Coast 2035, Traditional 2047 (not 2041). | Persistent banner while any section holds untouched example values, or open every section on first launch. Base the "yours"/"default" tag for claim ages on the actual value. |
| 3 | **Tax dated income.** Income items (pension, part-time work, rental, inherited IRA) are never taxed and don't shrink the Roth-ladder bracket room. The "enter after-tax" tooltip can't be followed: the right rate is the engine's own marginal rate. | Accuracy | $40k pension at 60 with 12% fill: tax understated **$10,800/yr**. A $30k pension entered gross makes the example one year earlier and ~$127k cheaper than it should be. | "Taxable as ordinary income" checkbox on income items (default on; off for home sales, cash gifts). Add to `ordinary` in `taxOf` and `existingOrdinary` for the bracket fill; same while working. |
| 4 | **Reword "Your date already allows for bad markets while you save."** The date is a probability seen from today. It is not permission to retire on that date whatever the balance. | Trust | Arriving in 2039 with the bad-market balance ($2.06M, not the $2.70M needed) gives **~63%** success, not 90%. | "If your savings in 2039 are below $2.70M, retiring then is below your 90% target. Re-run every year with your real balances." |
| 5 | **Roth ladder default and help text.** The 12% default does worse than 10% or off on the example plan, and the help text says it "builds early-retirement access". The conversions season the year the owner turns 60, so they add no early access. | Accuracy / trust | At 2039: **12%** needs $2.695M, penalty in 37.5% of markets, bad-market end $74k. **10%**: $2.622M, 19.0%, $365k. **Off**: $2.631M, 15.7%, $291k. | Fix the help text (access only if seasoned before 59½); add a "seasoned Roth available" year-table column; show the five fill options side by side (~0.7 s each). Default: see open decision 3. |

## Open decisions

These need a choice before building; the reviewers pull in different directions.

1. **Which way to correct.** Items 1, 3 and 10 move answers later; 6, 15, 22, 23 and 24 move them earlier.
   Fixing only the optimistic errors stacks conservative choices on top of the 90% target and Fidelity-style
   defaults. Fixing both keeps the answer honest but may move dates earlier than v1's.
2. **More inputs vs. fewer surprises.** Items 3, 7, 13 and 23 add inputs; item 2 shows the current input count
   already lets example values leak into results. Each new input needs a default that is safe to leave alone.
3. **Roth fill default.** On the example plan 12% is worse than 10% or off at the 90% target and at the median.
   But v1 doesn't model what conversions protect against (survivor single brackets, IRMAA, late-life RMD
   brackets), so optimizing the fill against v1's own metric biases toward converting too little. Options:
   change the default to 10%, auto-pick per plan, or keep 12% and show the comparison.
4. **Penalty paths as success.** Showing the penalty rate (item 8) is uncontroversial. Counting heavy penalty
   use as failure would move dates later; adding Rule of 55 (item 24) would move them earlier.
5. **Precision vs. hedging.** Items 4, 16 and 27 add caveats to the cards; the power-user view wants one crisp
   number plus comparison. Consider hedges on the card and precision in the detail view.
6. **Validation strictness.** Hard limits (item 20) prevent nonsense but block legitimate edge uses (debt as a
   negative balance, high-tax locales). Prefer warnings where a real case exists.
